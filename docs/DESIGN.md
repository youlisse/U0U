# DrumBox design

A plain drum machine for the M-VAVE FM-1, then a step-based effect section on its master bus (in the spirit of ShaperBox / Infiltrator). Started from Felucca 1.7.0 (GPL-3.0-only); `../Felucca/` is the untouched baseline to diff against.

Decisions taken: 16 steps over 4 beats (a 2-beat mode doubles the resolution), synthetic drums first and samples later, effects on the master bus only for v1, as step lanes (one effect per black key, 16 steps each).

## Keyboard mapping

The 27 note keys are `fm1_in.notes` bits 0..26, one per semitone from F3 (Felucca `docs/controls.jpg`, `fm1_input.h`: ids 14..40).

- **16 white keys** = the 16 steps. Semitone offsets from F3: `0 2 4 6 7 9 11 12 14 16 18 19 21 23 24 26`.
- **11 black keys** = the 11 instruments. Offsets: `1 3 5 8 10 13 15 17 20 22 25`.

| Black key | Instrument | `drum_voice.c` type |
|---|---|---|
| F#3 | KICK | `DVT_PUNCH` (`DVT_ROUND` as a variant) |
| G#3 | SNARE | `DVT_SNARE` |
| A#3 | CLAP | `DVT_CLAP` |
| C#4 | HAT CL | `DVT_HATC` |
| D#4 | HAT OP | `DVT_HATO` |
| F#4 | TOM | `DVT_TOM` |
| G#4 | CONGA | `DVT_CONGA` |
| A#4 | RIM | `DVT_RIM` |
| C#5 | CLAVE | `DVT_CLAVE` |
| D#5 | BELL | `DVT_BELL` |
| F#5 | CYM | `DVT_CYM` |

The lineup is a first guess; the voices already exist, nothing new to synthesize.

## Part 1: drum machine

- **Black key**: selects the instrument for editing. It does not play it.
- **White key**: toggles that step of the selected instrument (hold a step + turn a knob for velocity / accent later). LEDs show the selected instrument's steps and the playhead.
- **Subdivision**: 1/8 (16 steps over 8 beats), 1/16 (4 beats) or 1/32 (2 beats), set on the GLO page; the pattern keeps its 16 steps per lane.
- **Transport**: PLAY/STOP. Tempo, global volume, clock source and subdivision are on the first GLO page (knobs 1 to 4), swing and erase on the second. MASTER = volume pot.
- **Knobs 1..4** (page dependent): tune, decay, tone and level of the selected instrument.
- **REC**: live-record black keys into the current step.
- **Patterns**: a bank of patterns, picked with the PRESETS encoder. Saved with Felucca's project storage.
- **Mute / solo**: hold a button + black key, as Felucca does on its tracks.

One key held and another pressed copies the held pattern onto the other, which is then shown. **EDIT held 0.6 s on this page deletes the pattern shown**: the ones after it move down by one (with 3 patterns, deleting the 2nd leaves 1 and the old 3 as 2; the 16th becomes a new empty one), each with its steps, effects' steps, own step values and sound; the song loses its slots of that pattern and its later numbers follow; the number shown stays (it now shows the next pattern); what plays follows its pattern (the header says DELETED; the project's swing, ARP and mutes are not touched, which HOME held does reset). **Held 0.6 s**: everything that waits is written at once, one object a frame, even while playing (the header says SAVED); on the FM-1 a sector's erase may stop the audio for a moment, which is to be measured at M5. **The song page**: the 16 white keys are 16 slots; a slot holds a pattern and a repeat (KNOB 1 the pattern, `-` for the end of the chain; KNOB 2 its passes: 1, 2, 4 or 8), KNOB 3 **AGAIN** (0 to 100 %, 5 % a click: the chance that, once its passes are played, the slot plays them once more instead of the next slot, each time; 100 % holds the slot), KNOB 4 turns the SONG on or off (clockwise on). The tempo, the subdivision, the volume and the clock source are the transport's and stay. The header says RESET, and the autosave writes it a moment later (when stopped), so the next start loads that state |

The walk adds up: a chance that drifts low can silence a step for good. **Each lane has its own** AMOUNT, EVERY and DRIFT: the page shows the selected lane's, and a lane goes on mutating while another is shown (a kick that mutates keeps doing so when you open the hi-hat); SCOPE ALL writes the selected lane's values into every lane. The pages draw the lane's 16 steps on a ring (the hits, a line from one to the next, the step playing in red) |
| **REC** (tap: armed, again: away) | armed, the black keys **sound** their drum (the lane as it is set, at velocity 100) and, while playing, **write** it: the step the hit falls on if it came less than half a step after that step began, else the next one; a step already on stays as it is (an overdub: the existing hits are never touched), a new one starts as a step does and SELECT sets its velocity. Stopped, they only sound. On the FX pages the black keys are effects and nothing is played or written. The header says REC (red) and the LED is lit |
| **OCT-**, **OCT+** | the **two sections** of the pattern shown (a track of 16 steps, or of 32). A pattern has one at first: OCT- does nothing; OCT+ asks *DOUBLE THE TRACK?* on the screen and a second OCT+ does it: the first section's steps (its effects' steps, its steps' own values) are copied into a second, which is then shown. With two, OCT+ and OCT- go from one to the other (OCT+ in the second does nothing). OCT- in the first section asks *DELETE THE 2ND PART?* and a second OCT- takes it away. Any other key, the other OCT, or 4 s drop the question. The section shown is the one that is edited, whatever plays: the pattern plays its first section, then its second, and goes round (the header says `PATTERN 1A` or `1B`, the playhead is drawn only on the section that plays; PLAY starts from the section shown). A pattern is picked on the SAVE page (a doubled pattern has a bar under its number), where it waits for the end of its last section while playing. The second sections live in the pool (14 KB of steps, 3 KB of effects' steps, 5 KB of own values) and in seven flash objects of their own (OBJ_SECB0.., from 0xA0000); the flags of which pattern has one are in the first objects' spare word |
| **LFO** (tap: LFO 1, again: LFO 2, again: closes) | two LFOs, **LFO 1 on the volume, LFO 2 on the pan**, each with KNOB 1 **RATE** (a note value locked to the tempo, the length of one cycle: 4 BARS, 2 BARS, 1 BAR, 1/2, 1/4, 1/8, 1/16, 1/32), KNOB 2 **AMOUNT** (0 to 100 %, 5 % a click; **0 leaves the sound exactly as it is**), KNOB 3 **SHAPE** (SINE, TRIANGLE, RAMP UP, RAMP DOWN, SQUARE, RANDOM: a new value each cycle), KNOB 4 **PHASE** (0 to 345 degrees, 15 a click, going round). The cycles start with the sequencer (PLAY) and run on while it is stopped. Volume: the top of the shape is the sound as it is, the bottom AMOUNT less of it (RAMP UP at 0 degrees ducks on the beat and swells back: a pump; SQUARE a gate). Pan: up is right, down is left, AMOUNT how far; a balance (the centre and the near side stay at unity, so nothing gets louder or quieter at rest), and it moves **only what is above 100 Hz**: a Linkwitz-Riley crossover (24 dB an octave) keeps the kick's body in the middle and pans the rest (it was 300 Hz, and the toms and congas hardly moved: measured on the emulator at full amount, the far side of a conga is 6 dB down at 300 Hz and 37 dB at 100 Hz). **Both LFOs come before the effects**: LFO 1 at the head of the chain, on the drums' mono sum (so the delay's and the reverb's tails are not pumped), LFO 2 where the chain turns stereo (the chain is mono up to the PAN effect), after PAN and before FLANGER, DELAY, REPEAT, REVERB and SATURATE 2, which carry its motion. In place of the oscilloscope the page draws the LFO: two cycles as set (shape, phase and amount), a violet cursor running along them with a dot on the curve, and in the corners its value now (`VOLUME 56%`, `PAN L 79%`) and its rate. Defaults: LFO 1 at 1/8, LFO 2 at 1/2, both at 0 %. **Each pattern has its own** (they are part of its sound); they are also kept in the settings (in the spare bytes of the first eight lanes' kit records, stored + 1 so that a save from before reads as the defaults) as the live ones |
| **SCL** (tap: opens the page, tap again: closes it) | the master page, the last stage of the sound: KNOB 1 **HPF** (off at the left), KNOB 2 **LPF** (off at the right), KNOB 3 **OTT** dry / wet (a three-band compressor that also lifts what is quiet: low, mid and high split at 120 Hz and 2.5 kHz), KNOB 4 **CLIP**: the gain into a soft clipper (0 to +18 dB). At 0 it is off; turned up, the sound saturates softly and its peaks end exactly on 0 dBFS. Every knob at its end leaves the sound as it was |
| MASTER | volume (the pot) |

### The screen (240 x 240)

**The firmware is called U0U** (the folder and the source files keep the working name DrumBox). Its boot screen is three centred lines and nothing else: `U0U` (large), `By`, `@Y_oulisse`, for 900 ms from power-on (`DBX_SPLASH_MS`); the keys and knobs already work under it, and the page is drawn whole when it ends.

Every page has the same frame, top to bottom: the header (pattern, PLAY/STOP, BPM, and, left of the tempo, the **battery**: 3 bars from the ADC 3 reading with the stock firmware's thresholds 531 / 561 / 591, smoothed, red at one bar or none, a bolt in place of the bars when a USB host powers the box) at y 4, the page title (H1) at y 20, the **oscilloscope** (216 x 72, y 58 to 130), the 16 steps in one row (y 134, four groups of four), then four compact rows (a label and its value, a 3-px bar under them) from y 160. **Colours**: near black, the green `#c5f78b` (`DBX_GREEN`) for everything that is shown (the text, the values, the bars, the wave; the greys of labels and rules are the green's, derived by the palette), the violet `#b48cff` (`DBX_VIOLET`) for what is on or waiting: PLAY / LOOP, a pattern waiting, the mark of an edited step. The palette is set by `dbx_palette()` (the custom palette slot of `gfx.c`); no white is left on any page (a test checks it). The playhead is a whole cell lit in red `#ff4646` (`DBX_RED_PLAY`) with its bar in a dark red, the bar kept 5 px short of the top so that a step at full velocity (or full mix) does not hide it. The pattern page replaces the steps and rows with its 16 pattern cells.

The oscilloscope shows the output after the ceiling, so it is what leaves the box (ring of 512 samples, one in four of the left channel; it triggers on a rising zero crossing so a steady note holds still; the scale follows the peak, quick up and slow down, with a floor so that silence stays a flat line). Dotted marks at the beats, a filled wave with a 2-px trace. In its corners: on the left `STEP n  VEL v` (or `MIX m` on an effect page) when a step is picked, on the right the resolution (`1/16  4 BEATS`). It is drawn into a strip and sent in one blit every 30 ms (about 33 a second) and the rest of the screen only when something changes. **On the FM-1 the blit is about 20 ms of SPI, so its height (72) and its rate are the first things to tune at M5.**

LEDs: the selected instrument's black key, the white keys of its steps (the playhead inverts its key), PLAY's green while playing, GLO, EDIT or SAVE while their page is open. The screen shows each step as a bar whose height is its velocity, slid sideways by its nudge and cut where it leaves the cell (a late note is cut at the left, an early one at the right), a small mark on the steps that are edited, and the picked step (the one SELECT and EDIT work on) outlined in violet with a violet bar under its cell, so that a full-velocity bar, green to the edge, does not hide it. The NUDGE row of the EDIT page draws the same thing at the size of a step. The patterns start empty. On the pattern page the key of the pattern shown is lit, the ones that hold something are at the mid level.

How a step plays: the sequencer queues the hits of each step one step ahead (a nudge can put a hit before its step), each with the time it plays at; CHANCE is rolled when the step is queued, and PITCH moves that one hit (the lane's TUNE is the centre). Previewing (placing a step, or turning a knob on the EDIT page while stopped) plays the step as set, without nudge or chance.

### The help

A **tap of SEQ** opens a three-sheet help that fits the 240 x 240 screen: BUTTONS (what each does), HOLDS (the gestures held or combined: HOME, EDIT, SAVE held, GLO + key, SEQ held, SEQ + KNOB 1, the key kinds), KNOBS (SELECT, ALGO, PRESETS, K1 to K4, MASTER, the GLO 2 reset, the kick's TUNE as a note). A tap goes to the next sheet, the third closes it, HOME closes it at once. SEQ held (a quarter of a second) is still the loop, and SEQ + KNOB 1 is not a tap.

### Patterns and saving (M2)

- **A pattern** is the 11 lanes x 16 steps and each step's velocity, nudge, subdivision, chance and pitch (902 bytes). The tempo, the subdivision, the volume, the swing and ARP and the pattern shown are the project's, shared and saved apart.
- **The sound is the project's**, the same under every pattern: the kit and every lane's tune, decay, tone, extra, level, accent, pan and choke group (not the mute); every effect's 8 parameters and the FX MIX; the master page (HPF, LPF, OTT, CLIP); the two LFOs (RATE, AMOUNT, SHAPE, PHASE); ENV (its 8 values); with the tempo, the subdivision, the volume and the swing. What a **pattern** has of its own is its steps (the drums', the effects', and the steps' own values) and its **mutation** (ARP 2: AMOUNT, EVERY, DRIFT of each lane). The sound is saved as it was before this, a snapshot of 222 bytes, written 16 times in one object (`DBXU`, then the song's 40 bytes, OBJ_UPFM6's pair of sectors, 0x9F000 / 0xFE000) so that an older save is read as the sound of the pattern it showed.
- **Where**: the storage layer of Felucca (`storage.c`): every object is an A/B pair of 4 KB sectors, a save goes to the copy that is not current and the commit record (magic, sequence, CRC) is written last, so a power cut leaves the previous copy. The settings are one object (0xFC000), the patterns four objects of four patterns (0x97000 to 0x9EFFF), the effects one object (0xDC000: their parameters, the master page's four settings and the effect steps of the 16 patterns, 3264 bytes). Both carry a magic of their own: what an earlier firmware left in those sectors is ignored, and overwritten by the first save.
- **When**: 1.5 s after the last change, one object a frame, and **never while playing** (erasing a sector stops the audio on the FM-1; the wait starts again at the stop, so the last hits ring out). The header says SAVED for a moment. Nothing is written when nothing changed.
- **Loading** checks every value (a damaged or alien object is ignored, not trusted).

## Part 2: step effects (master bus)

The effects work like the instruments: **one effect per black key, 16 steps on the white keys**, on the FX pages. **FX** opens the first page (a tap), the second page (the next tap), then closes. A black key (or the ALGORITHM knob) picks an effect, a white key sets a step of it, SELECT sets the step's mix, PRESETS scrolls through its steps, KNOBS 1 to 4 set its parameters. The steps belong to the pattern (saved and copied with it, erased with it); the parameters are shared.

The keys are in the order of the signal, except the two filters. HALFTIME, BITCRUSH and SATURATE 1 work on the mono sum of the drums, PAN turns it into stereo, then FLANGER, DELAY, REPEAT (just before the reverb), REVERB and SATURATE 2; **LOW PASS and BAND PASS are last**, on the stereo output, so a filter on a step shapes everything that plays then: the echoes, the tails, and what the lanes' pans add (they sat ahead of the delay, which echoed the unfiltered sound, and the pans' side went round them). The master page (HPF, LPF, OTT) and the soft limiter come after all of them; the ceiling is 0 dBFS, and a peak that would pass it is bent by the limiter's knee (from -6 dBFS) instead of cut flat; the FM-1's help (third sheet) counts the samples that went past (`OVER`). The MASTER pot tops at **-3 dB** (a drum loop sits near 0 dBFS and a delay adds 1 to 4 dB to it: a loop with a feedback delay at the top of the pot was 3 dB over the ceiling and crackled; it now peaks under 0 dBFS).

|---|---|---|
| F#3 | **HALFTIME** | SPEED (down to a quarter), GRAIN, TONE, GAIN (a pitch shifter on a delay line: it slows and falls an octave at 50 %) |

| A#3 | **SATURATE 1** | DRIVE, BIAS (even harmonics), TONE, LEVEL: a tube, tanh |
| C#4 | **LOW PASS** | CUTOFF, RESO, FROM (where the sweep starts), SLOPE (12 / 24 dB) |
| D#4 | **BAND PASS** | CENTER, WIDTH, FROM, GAIN: the centre sweeps from FROM to CENTER |
| F#4 | **PAN** | POSITION, RATE (auto-pan, off at 0), DEPTH, SHAPE (sine / triangle / square) |
| G#4 | **FLANGER** | RATE, DEPTH, FEEDBACK, SPREAD (the two sides out of phase) |
| A#4 | **DELAY** | TIME (1/16, 1/8, 3/16, 1/4, 3/8, 1/2, synced), FEEDBACK, PING PONG (on: the echoes alternate left and right; off: the same echo on both sides), SPREAD (on: how far apart; off: how much later the right echo comes, up to half a step) |
| C#5 | **REPEAT** | DIV (1, 2 or 3: the slice is the step divided by it), DECAY (each pass quieter), TONE, REVERSE. A beat repeat on the stereo sound: it takes the slice from the step where it opens, that slice goes through as it is, then it is played over and over (so put it on the same step as the hit); with CONTINUOUS it takes a new slice at every step, and while stopped it lets the sound be |
| D#5 | **REVERB** | SIZE, DAMP, PRE (pre-delay), WIDTH |
| F#5 | **SATURATE 2** | DRIVE, FOLD (a hard clip, then the wave folds back), TONE, LEVEL: on the stereo sound, after the reverb |

**Second page, the same for every effect**: ATTACK, RELEASE, CONTINUOUS (on at every step, and while stopped), MIX (that effect's share).

**Where a step starts**: its mix is 100 % for every effect (SELECT moves it). The DELAY's and the REVERB's amount is their own MIX (page 2, KNOB 4), which starts at 40 % for the delay (its echo is at half the send's level, -6 dB: it was 3/4, and the echoes stood out) and 20 % for the reverb (whose SIZE and WIDTH start at 20 % too: a short, narrow room; an older save keeps the 30 % it had), the others' at 100 %: it is the amount of the whole effect, not of each step. (A save from before, with the steps at 50 and 30, is read into this: those steps become 100 and the MIX 50 and 30 %; steps set by hand stay.)

**The envelope**: a step that is on opens the effect through its attack, a step that is off closes it through its release. Steps in a row do not retrigger: **the attack is on the first step of the run and the release after the last**. The amount at any moment is that envelope x the step's own mix (SELECT) x the effect's MIX x the global FX MIX (GLO page 2, KNOB 2).

**What the amount does**: LOW PASS and BAND PASS sweep their cutoff from FROM to the target with it; DELAY and REVERB open their send (the tails ring on after it closes); REPEAT, HALFTIME, BITCRUSH, the saturators and FLANGER fade their wet sound in over the dry one; PAN moves the sound towards its position.

**Cost**: all eleven on at once cost about three times the drums alone in the browser engine (0.21 % of real time against 0.07 %); the cost on the FM-1's core is still to measure (M5). An effect with nothing to do costs a test.

### The master stage

After the effects: HPF, LPF, OTT, then the soft clipper (the gain into it is CLIP), then the volume, then the ceiling.

**The output ceiling is 0 dBFS**, soft clip off or on: the last stage is a clip at full scale (32767 of 32768, which is 0 dBFS in 24 bits), so nothing goes over it. With the soft clip off a sound that is too loud is cut flat at that level; with it on the saturation is a smooth knee (linear up to half scale, then tanh) that ends exactly on the same level. The MASTER pot is a fader after all this: at the top the peak is 0 dBFS. (Felucca kept its ceiling at -6 dBFS; DrumBox uses the whole range. To check on the FM-1's analog stage at M5.) It is saved with the effects (four bytes in their object's spare word, the LPF stored as 127 minus its value so that a save from before the page reads as off).

### The kits

No sample is stored: Felucca's `drum_voice.c` already holds eight **model kits** (called in the code 80, 10, 66, 55, 77, DEEP, TIGHT, PERC; shown as ANALOG, MINI, VINTAGE, METAL, DISCO, DEEP, TIGHT, PERC, names given by how they sound, `DBX_KIT_NAME`; CLASSIC is Felucca's own STD) (every lane a kit's own synthesised voice, from the numbers of `DV_KIT`) next to its own drums (STD). U0U plays them on its 11 lanes (`dbx_type_of` in `dbx_voices.c`): a model kit has 8 pieces (KICK SNARE CLAP HAT CL HAT OP TOM RIM BELL), some of which are another drum (kit 10 has a cymbal on BELL, 66 a conga on TOM, 77 claves on RIM and a cymbal on BELL, DEEP a cymbal, PERC a conga and claves); the lane of that name takes it, and the lane it leaves (TOM, RIM, BELL, CONGA, CLAVE or CYM) keeps STD's drum. A kit change starts every lane over with its kit's designed DECAY, TONE and TUNE (LEVEL and the mute are kept) and is taken up by the audio render, not the UI. The kick's TUNE is a note in every kit (the kit's own pitch, one octave each way).

Samples (an ADPCM kit from the internet) are possible later (Felucca has `adpcm_dsp.c`), but the flash is small (1 MB, of which the image takes 513 KB: the room left is to be measured at M5) and a sample pack's licence has to allow redistribution in a GPL firmware: CC0 or public domain only.

## Architecture

Felucca is one compilation unit (`firmware/src/felucca.c` includes every `.c` in order). DrumBox gets its own root, `firmware/src/drumbox.c`, which includes only what it needs:

- **Kept as is**: the HAL (`firmware/bsp/`, MPL-2.0), `lcd.c`, `gfx.c`, fonts and icons, `dsp.c`, `drum_voice.c`, `midi_clock.c`, `fx.c` buses (delay, reverb), `storage*.c` and the OTA entry (`ota_hw.c`, `bsp/chip/ota.c`).
- **Dropped**: the 14 synth engines, the 4 tracks, arpeggiator, chords, FM6, slicer UI, the editor protocol.
- **New**: `dbx_seq.c` (patterns, step clock), `dbx_ui.c` (pages, keys, LEDs, screen), `dbx_fx.c` (the 11 step effects), `dbx_lfo.c` (the two LFOs), `dbx_snap.c` (the project's sound, as a snapshot to save), `dbx_arp.c` (Euclid and mutation), `dbx_audio.c` (render block: 11 voices -> mix (the middle) and side (what the lanes' pans add) -> LFO 1 -> step effects, the mono ones on the middle, then the side joins (left = middle + side, right = middle - side), LFO 2 where the chain is stereo -> master). The mono effects (HALFTIME, BITCRUSH, SATURATE 1, LOW PASS, BAND PASS) therefore see the middle only: a lane panned hard has half of its level in them and its side goes past them. The LFOs' gains ramp inside each block of 32 frames, so a square shape does not click.
- **Emulator**: `web/emu/drumbox_web.c`, a trimmed copy of `felucca_web.c` (simulated clock, panel, LCD, flash). It is the main dev loop; hardware comes last.
- **Build**: `tools/build.py` gets a target switch (`felucca.c` vs `drumbox.c`), and the generated tables only include what DrumBox uses.

## Budgets

**Flash and RAM** (from the Felucca V1.7.0 build): image 513 KB, RAM `.data+.bss` 61.8 of 73.7 KB, pool 326 of 368 KB. Dropping the engines frees most of the pool; U0U's own buffers (the effects' delay, reverb, repeat, halftime and flanger lines, the scope's snapshot) are about 296 KB of the pool's 368 KB. The RAM figure is not known until the build for the board (M5).

**CPU**: the FM-1's core runs at 360 MHz, 8160 cycles per sample at 44.1 kHz. `web/emu/bench_native.c` builds the audio path with `cc` and counts instructions per sample the way Felucca's `tests/cpu_baseline.txt` does (`/usr/bin/time -l`, `web_test_heavy(level)`: 0 idle, 1 all 11 lanes on every step at 1/32, 2 and all 11 effects always on at full MIX, 3 and both LFOs, the master page's HPF, LPF, OTT and soft clip). Measured on the Mac: about 200 at rest, +650 for the drums, +680 for the effects, +510 for the LFOs, the master page, ENV (ducking, attack, gate), a pan on every lane and the mutation: **about 2050 instructions a sample at the worst**, against 1574 for Felucca's heaviest reference scene (`cpu/mix/3parts_full_drums`) and 1202 for its ANALOG SAW LEAD at 8 voices, which it measured at 17 % of the CPU on the FM-1 (so about 1.15 cycles an instruction: our worst case about 29 %; with the 2.6 of its ADPCM engine, the slowest known, about 65 %). A real scene is far lighter than this worst case. To be measured on the board at M5 with Felucca's `cpu_pct` meter, and shown on the screen.

## Safety

The OTA re-entry (`FELUCCA_OTA`, `ota_hw.c`) must stay, or a flashed DrumBox cannot be replaced over MIDI (see README, Flashing and safety). Package identity is `FM-1_2100003` (210 for U0U, 0003 for 0.3, 0002 was 0.2, 0001 was 0.1; free on the Firmware Hub's identity check; Felucca's is `FM-1_900`). Always back up the device first.

## Milestones

1. **M0** (done) DrumBox skeleton builds in the emulator; a black key plays its drum.
2. **M1** (done) Step clock and sequencer, 16 steps on the white keys, tempo, LEDs, screen. The resolution toggle (SEQ) came with it.
3. **M2** (done) The 11 instruments with their parameters, 16 patterns and saving.
4. **M3** (done) Live REC (swing and the MIDI clock were done before).
5. **M4** (done) Step effects (11, one per black key) and their pages. Since: the LFO, ENV, ARP, kits, OCT patterns, master reset.
6. **M5** Hardware build, then the submission to The Firmware Hub (<https://thefirmwarehub.com/submit/>), with a demo video, in this order:
   1. **Hardware build** (built; to run on the board): `firmware/src/drumbox.c` is the root (the HAL, the drum voices, the `dbx_*` files, USB and the update entry; no engines, one core, no CDC / UAC / TRS), `dbx_main.c` its boot and main loop (`web_frame` of the browser build plus the ways out), `firmware/drumbox.ld` its memory map (RAM 56 KB, POOL 376 KB: the effects' lines fill the pool). `tools/build.py` builds it by default (`--target felucca`: the original) and writes `build/u0u.fwsc`: app 134 KB, RAM 47 of 56 KB, pool 360 of 376 KB. The CPU is read on the board: the help's third sheet shows `LOAD n%  PEAK m%`. Guide: `docs/FLASHING.md`.
   2. **The way out**: the OTA re-entry (`ota_hw.c`) works in the U0U build, so a flashed U0U can be replaced.
   3. **Back up the FM-1** and keep the stock `.fwsc` (README, Flashing and safety), then **flash it**: it needs the real machine, which is the user's.
   4. **Test on the real FM-1**: install from V15 and from another firmware, and leave U0U again. The hub lists nothing untested; the emulator does not count.
   5. **Identity and licence**: an `FM-1_` number nobody else uses (not `FM-1_900`, which is Felucca's; the hub has an "Is my identity free?" check), and the licence settled in the README (GPL-3.0).
   6. **GitHub**: a public repository with the source at the tag, and a release with the `.fwsc` attached (the hub picks new releases up within three hours).
   7. **A page to try it and to install it, on GitHub Pages**: one site, `site/` in the repository (`python3 web/make_site.py build/u0u.fwsc 0.3 site --repo https://github.com/youlisse/U0U`, after `web/emu/build_drumbox.sh`): the emulator at the root (`index.html`, `felucca.wasm`, `worklet.js`, `fonts/`, with an "Install it on your FM-1" button), the installer at `install/` (self-contained, it fetches `firmware/u0u-0.3.fwsc`, which travels with the licences). Published from the `gh-pages` branch, which holds `site/` as its root: `tools/publish_pages.sh` (`git subtree push --prefix site origin gh-pages`). The root is the `play` link of `fmhub.json`.
   8. **A demo video**: U0U playing on the real FM-1 (a beat on the white keys, the drums on the black ones, an effect placed on steps, EDIT per step, a pattern chain), short, hosted by a video site; its link goes in the email (the hub takes optional video links) and in the README.
   9. **`fmhub.json`** at the repository root: `what` (plain English, 300 characters at most, no links), `kinds` (`drums`), `author`, `play` (the page to try it), and a square `image` (240 to 1024 px, PNG, WebP or JPEG, 300 KB at most).
   10. **The email** to zven@thefirmwarehub.com, with links and no attachments (name, author, repository, `FM-1_` number, licence, what it does, picture, the page to try it, the video, tested on a real FM-1): sent by the user, never by Claude.
7. **M6** Samples (ADPCM slots, as `eng_sample.c`).
